## 1. The core argument
The problem in classrooms — especially high-poverty ones — is not student motivation but suppressed or under-developed agency. Beneath agency lies a substrate, intellectual authorship: the capacity to treat one's own thinking about a subject as real and consequential — to author meaning within content one did not choose. That capacity is built through an intellectual apprenticeship: a deliberate, late, in-school rebuilding — for students who were not given it early or elsewhere — of the apprenticeship in authoring meaning that more advantaged students received informally. Motivation locates the problem in the student; this book relocates it to whether the capacity to author meaning was ever apprenticed, and whether the teacher can perceive it.
## 2. The naming, resolved
The practice has ONE name: intellectual apprenticeship. The capacity it builds is intellectual authorship. Everything else names an action that serves the apprenticeship:
- the knower-stance — the commitment it rests on (students are knowers, not learners-in-waiting);
- two-register teaching — the design move (a shared object plus a dialogic register run alongside it);
- the curated / perceptible object — the first thing the apprenticeship requires;
- respect-based differentiation, the four-stage progression, the skull station, two-things-per-class, double booking — the concrete forms the apprenticeship takes in a room.
These are facets of one thing described from several angles, not competing labels. Capstone title that encodes the resolution: The Apprenticeship in Intellectual Authorship.
"Authorial agency" vs. "intellectual authorship": authorial agency is the rare outermost form of the taxonomy (Marcus — authoring one's path outside the frame). Intellectual authorship is the foundational capacity to author meaning within imposed content, beneath the whole taxonomy. Credit Matusov's notion of mature authorship; mark the departure — he describes the mature form, this book stages the apprenticeship that precedes it.
## 3. The agency taxonomy
Shadow forms: dysfunctional (the last visible form of insisting something is at stake), pseudo (activity without ownership; "agency without authorship"), effortful (meeting external expectations; real but not the goal). Functional forms: engaged (effort to understand from one's own position), epistemic (a bid to reshape the frame), democratic (shaping the conditions of shared classroom life), authorial (setting one's own learning path outside the frame). Intellectual authorship is the substrate; the taxonomy is its visible expression. The forms are a perceptual vocabulary, not a checklist or a ladder.
## 4. Load-bearing conceptual decisions
- Agency is a developmental capacity, not a trait. Built from the outside in; unevenly distributed by the conditions that sort children before they reach school. (Grounded in the EF/SES literature — §10.)
- Autonomy =/= agency. Offering autonomy before the substrate exists produces avoidance/anxiety, not agency (the "stations" — half to two-thirds ignored them; the thesis demonstrated). Critique the naive reading of SDT from inside SDT: autonomy support and structure are synergistic; it is autonomy-thwarting and directly controlling behavior that harms, not structure.
- Inquiry is a consequence of teaching, not a precondition. "Inquiry has prerequisites." The field treats inquiry as either a precondition (progressive) or an absence to be motivated (traditional); both assume it is brought or lacked, neither asks where it comes from. Right teaching generates it. "Not the fuel you need to start the engine; the exhaust of an engine that is running."
- The five apprenticeship movements (see §5).
- Critical-aspects practice: build a lesson around 2–3 critical aspects that shape understanding — found by locating the load-bearing misconception — each tied to a "play-around" discernment activity built to isolate that aspect. Worked example: natural selection = death + differential reproduction / population-not-individual / variation-came-first, NOT individual striving or learning.
- Two axes, kept separate: (1) phases of engagement — lurking / messing around / geeking out — co-occur, not a ladder; (2) mode of representation — enactive → iconic → symbolic — recursive and domain-relative (holds for adults; Bruner). Link: messing-around with unfamiliar material takes the enactive form.
- Learning styles reframed as developmental positions — where a student is now, not what they are. (Pashler 2008; Nancekivell 2020; a 2024 meta-analysis she must supply.)
- Control has a developmental cost (original contribution): withholding hands-on exploration for behavioral control forecloses the enactive substrate ("can't use a ruler" = deprivation, not deficit). The management practice that keeps the room orderly keeps the substrate from forming.
- Patall's backfire = the empirical signature of the missing substrate (see §10, and the drafted convergence passage).
- Agentic engagement =/= intellectual authorship. Agentic engagement (shaping the instruction you receive) is the visible outer layer; authoring meaning comes first. Do not let the two collapse into synonyms — the argument depends on the distinction.

## 5. The five apprenticeship movements
Not a strict ladder; they recur and overlap. Each pulls a seed from an earlier chapter.
1. Perceptible object — make the critical aspect discernible before asking for meaning. (Variation Theory; Montessori materials isolating one variable.)
2. Joint attention & proximity — attend with the student; lend the footing they lack. (Vygotsky's co-regulation; the Germantown proximity finding.)
3. Legitimate peripheral participation — partial, low-stakes, "wrong" participation counts as a beginning. (Lave & Wenger; Ito's lurking/messing around.)
4. Dialogic answerability — a tentative meaning is received as a bid and made answerable; a bid becomes authorship only when someone receives it as one. (Matusov; Bakhtin's internally persuasive discourse / answering word.)
5. Graduated transfer — the scaffold fades; teacher → shared → self-regulation. The apprentice is a knower from day one, not a novice graduating into knowing. (Vygotsky; cognitive-apprenticeship fading; the four-stage progression.)
## 6. Scoping & robustness rules
Apply whenever a claim could overreach:
- Proof-of-possibility, not confirmation. The next-year classroom study is a source of hypotheses, not a confirmatory test (one teacher, one year, no control, novel construct). It cannot distinguish a measurement problem from a theory problem, so claim accordingly.
- Two layers. The diagnostic half rests on the synthesis and outside evidence (Patall; EF/SES; curriculum-narrowing) and survives a flat result. The prescriptive half (scaffolding authorship yields more agency) is what a flat result would dent. Keep them separate.
- Scope "achievable" to its level. The classroom shift is achievable now, by teachers; the system shift is achievable in principle but needs the upstream levers (pacing, the Carnegie unit, accountability, credits) to move, which are not in a teacher's hands. Don't let a classroom-level cure carry a system-level promise.
- Hold "unmapped" to its defensible version. The missing reframe is a precondition for change, not the sole cause of inertia — pacing/control/credits persist because they serve real functions (Berliner's "rational response" shows people often understand the harm and do it anyway). The conceptual lever is one among several (interests, incentives, structures).
- Pre-commit, in writing, before the posttest, to what result would change her mind and what the design cannot show.
- Tool-constraint history — narrow only. Assert: in my classroom, offloading production let me hold knower-stance grading + differentiation + presence at once. Decline: AI will dissolve the field's dichotomies at scale.
- Honesty notes. Lean on the developmental claim (new material is met concretely first); the prescriptive concrete-then-abstract sequencing claim has limited empirical support. Keep individual student reads light; let population-level patterns carry the weight, with the individual as illustration.
- Social-promotion scope (Harris 2020): gains from ending grade retention are mostly real, not diploma inflation — scope to the experiential level only.
## 7. The AI framing (her words)
AI on a content-filling pedagogy harms (hardest for the already-harmed); AI on a knower-supporting pedagogy serves. "It helps make it doable; it is not the doer." Human-in-the-loop: she reads and adjusts all AI feedback before it reaches students; AI without human interaction is insufficient for learning. Reversed split: AI handles ~40% (production), returning time → an honest estimate of a 20–60% increase in her presence (not data). No platform-selling — her use is idiosyncratic. Focus on high-poverty students; the doctoral-seminar parallel is cut. Chapter title in use: Doable, Not the Doer.
