This is the durable record that must resist getting stale. New conceptual insights, fusion thoughts, author decisions, recovered material, and important interpretive developments go here first. A manuscript revision, fusion pass, chapter replacement, or compression pass must check the conceptual/version-trail canon before removing, merging, or materially simplifying a developed point in the Working Copy. When a new insight is developed in conversation, bank it in **Full added notes** before treating a chapter as caught up. When chapter prose changes, keep the conceptual trail rather than rewriting history to match the newest prose.

If the two sources appear to conflict, Katherine's newest explicit ruling controls. Do not silently reconcile a conceptual difference.

## Fusion-pass implication

A fusion pass asks both sources different questions:

- From **Full added notes**: What distinctions, mechanisms, pressure tests, and emergent syntheses has Katherine developed that must not disappear?
- From the **Working Copy**: What prose and structure currently carry those ideas, where are genuine redundancies, and where is fusion still implicit?

The pass then proposes or applies only the level of change Katherine has authorized. It never uses apparent absence from the Working Copy as evidence that a developed idea was rejected if that idea remains live in the conceptual canon.
