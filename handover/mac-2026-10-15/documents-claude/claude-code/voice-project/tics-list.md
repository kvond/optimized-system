# AI Tics List — Hard Bans

Derived from ten unstyled 500-word AI drafts on Katherine's topics (5,108 words), compared against her verified-clean prose (7,330 words). Counts are (AI baseline / Katherine).

## Lexical bans

- T1. **genuine / genuinely** (14 / 0) — "designing tasks with genuine openings"
- T2. **quiet / quietly as drama adverb** (9 / 2) — "Fourth, relevance decays quietly." (Literal uses describing an actual quiet person or room are fine.)
- T3. **runs deeper / goes deeper / deeper truth** (5 / 0) — "Agency runs deeper: it is the felt sense that…"
- T4. **felt sense** — "the felt sense that one's actions matter"
- T5. **masquerading as** — "compliance masquerading as engagement"
- T6. **precisely as intensifier** (3 / 1) — "These are precisely the metacognitive habits…" ("be precise about" in her scale-check sense is allowed.)
- T7. **meaningful / meaningfully as filler praise** — "meaningful choices," "the meaningful work of making sense"
- T8. **Classic AI vocabulary**: delve, crucial, pivotal, foster, robust, landscape (figurative), tapestry, testament, journey (figurative), navigate (figurative), unpack, nuanced, holistic, transformative, invaluable, "rich" as in "genuinely rich questions"
- T9. **Reach-for-profundity noun phrases**: "hidden curriculum of passivity," "quiet mismatch," "the work is theirs," "authors of their learning." (Exception, Katherine's ruling Aug 22, 2026: "arithmetic of exhaustion" is author-adopted — allowed, once per chapter, where it does definitional work.)

## Structural bans

- T10. **"It is not X; it is Y" with an abstract Y** (both halves abstractions) — "Engagement is not the absence of disruption; it is the presence of investment." *Guard: Katherine's own reversal move is allowed up to twice per article when the second term is concrete or physical ("the exhaust of an engine that is running"). Ban applies only to abstraction-paired versions. These two rules never conflict: check the right-hand term — picturable = allowed, abstract = banned.*
- T11. **First, / Second, / Third, / Finally, as paragraph or sentence openers** (5 / 0) — "First, motivation."
- T12. **Enumerated-culprit template** — "The first culprit is… The second culprit is…"
- T13. **None of this means…** (3 / 0) — "None of this means classroom management is beside the point."
- T14. **Anthropomorphized abstractions** — "the guide whispers that there is no time," "the system asks," "the curriculum insists"
- T15. **Rule-of-three catalogs as default rhythm** — "weighing alternatives, justifying choices, monitoring one's own understanding." One triad per article at most; if two drafts of a sentence both come out as triads, rebuild the sentence with two items or four.
- T16. **Anaphora runs** — three or more consecutive sentences opening with the same words: "They learn to read… They learn to decode… They learn to participate…"
- T17. **Title-case colon headline** — "Student Agency in the Classroom: What It Is and Why It Matters"
- T18. **Tidy kicker close** — a final paragraph that restates the piece in elevated register: "…and come to see themselves as people whose thinking matters."
- T19. **Generic anecdote with no named person** — "There is a moment familiar to every teacher: scanning a room of twenty-five faces…"
- T20. **Balanced-antithesis flourish** — "whether they show up in the ways that matter, or simply show up"
- T21. **Metronomic rhythm** — sentence-length standard deviation below ~10 words across the piece; almost no fragments, almost no questions, no 30+-word sentences. (This is a measured ban, not a phrase ban.)
- T22. **Em-dash saturation** — more than ~3–4 dashes per 1,000 words. Katherine's ruling: heavy em-dash use is residue from AI editing sweeps, not her voice — her dictated raw prose has zero em dashes in 682 words, and she types `--` sparingly. (AI baseline: 5.9/1000; AI-swept manuscript files: 16–20/1000.) A couple of dashes per article is the ceiling.

## Non-tics (do not "fix" these)

- Colons in prose. Hers: 13% of sentences.
- Semicolon chains inside long builds.
- Fragments.
