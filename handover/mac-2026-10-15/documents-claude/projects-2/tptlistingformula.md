# TPT Listing Formula — Variation Theory Biology Lessons

Reusable template for optimizing each Variation Theory (VT) lesson listing on Teachers Pay Teachers. Apply this to one unit at a time, following the units-first sequencing decision (individual listings build search presence and reviews before any bundle is created).

Sources: TPT seller SEO guidance (title/keyword/description structure, current as of 2026) and the promotional brief already drafted for Manus.ai, which correctly identifies the differentiator as the pedagogy itself, not generic slide aesthetics.

---

## 1. The hook: why "critical aspects" is the differentiator

A critical aspect, in Variation Theory, is the one specific feature of a concept a student must notice in order to understand it — the feature that, if missed, means the concept was never actually grasped. Most TPT slide decks present information. These lessons are built so students discern the critical aspect: each one is named up front, and the slide sequence (contrast sets, a build-a-rule step, a pattern-break, and a three-tier concept question) exists to make that aspect impossible to miss.

Ten-second version for a listing or social post: "You'll know exactly what each lesson is teaching students to *see* — and so will they."

Revised after pushback: the research-based/evidence-based positioning should lead, not follow. There is a real and growing population of biology teachers actively seeking instruction grounded in cognitive science — reducing cognitive load, retrieval practice, teaching students what to actually notice rather than piling on more content — and that audience is large enough to be the headline claim, not a closing credibility line. The narrower point that still holds: the literal term "Variation Theory" has close to no TPT search volume, since a teacher unfamiliar with the term won't type it into the search box. That is a reason to keep the bare term out of tag slots specifically (a mechanical search-matching decision), not a reason to soften the research-based claim itself, which belongs in the title subtitle and the opening line of the description.

## 2. Title formula

`[Topic/Standard] + [Grade/Course] + [Format] + [Differentiator phrase]`

Example shape (topic to be filled in once the first unit is chosen):
`Mitosis vs. Meiosis | High School Biology Slides | Research-Based, Not Just Decoration`

Notes:
- Lead with the exact term a teacher would type into TPT search (the topic/standard) — that's still the first thing indexed and scanned.
- Follow it with the research-based/evidence-based claim as the subtitle, not buried in the description — this is the actual differentiator and the audience for it is large.
- Keep the full title under roughly 70 characters where possible so it doesn't truncate in search results.
- "Research-based" / "evidence-based" / "conceptual understanding" / "cognitive science" are legitimate keywords here because the product actually delivers them — this is not keyword-stuffing, it's accurate, and it's a real, searched-for category.

## 3. Description structure

Opening (first two lines carry the most weight — many buyers never scroll past them):
1. What the resource is, who it's for, and what skill it teaches — one plain sentence.
2. The research-based claim stated directly, then the critical-aspects hook as the mechanism behind it: this lesson is built on Variation Theory, a framework for what students must actually notice to understand a concept — not just another slide deck.

Body (scannable, short sections or bullets — not paragraphs):
- What's included (slide count, format — Google Slides/PowerPoint, digital activity type).
- The named critical aspect(s) this specific lesson targets (this is the part competitors cannot copy without also doing the pedagogical work).
- The beat structure in plain terms a teacher recognizes: contrast set → build-a-rule → pattern-break → three-tier concept check (name only the beats this lesson actually uses; not every lesson carries all seven beats, so don't claim ones it doesn't).
- Format specs: 4:3, Arial, teal accent (#028090), white-background design — worth stating explicitly, since "clean, non-cluttered slides" is itself a buyer search filter.
- Prep needed / how it's used (whole class, stations, asynchronous).

Close:
- One line connecting to your author identity: a working teacher writing a research-based book on student agency, currently publishing chapters and classroom systems at [answerable-teaching.netlify.app](https://answerable-teaching.netlify.app/) and on Substack. This is a credibility signal, not a hard sell — per your existing bio conventions, "thirty years K–12 teaching, adjunct doctoral faculty," never "Title I" or "high-poverty" in public copy.

## 4. Tag / keyword set (apply per unit, adjust topic term)

Mix of high-volume and long-tail so the listing is findable both by browsers and by specific searchers:
- Topic term exactly as teachers search it (e.g., "mitosis meiosis," "punnett squares," "DNA replication")
- `[topic] high school biology`
- `[topic] google slides` or `[topic] powerpoint`
- `research based biology lesson`
- `evidence based biology teaching`
- `conceptual biology lessons`
- `differentiated biology slides`
- `biology critical thinking activity`
- `no-prep biology lesson`

Avoid spending a tag slot on the bare term "Variation Theory" — it has close to zero search volume because most buyers haven't encountered the term yet. That is strictly a tag-matching decision, not a reason to soften the claim: "research-based" and "evidence-based" carry the same underlying differentiator and do have real search volume, so they take the tag slots the raw theory name would otherwise waste.

## 5. Commercial image constraint — do not skip

Every image in the listing thumbnail, preview, and inside the product itself must be cleared for commercial use: CC0/public domain, or CC BY with visible attribution. No NonCommercial-licensed images anywhere in a sold product. This is the piece currently in progress in the separate image-clearance tool — no listing should go live until that pass is complete for the unit being published.
