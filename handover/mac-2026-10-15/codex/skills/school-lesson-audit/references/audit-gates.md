# Complete lesson audit gates

## 1. Identity, pacing and source coverage

Compare the Agenda's daily teaching plan with topic label, master title, actual contents, week folder and materials. A lesson that spans weeks may need a range; an assessment or source container is not automatically a lesson. Record closure, first-week attendance/A-B-day exceptions and this-year-only repetition in teacher-only guidance. Do not erase what students already encountered or infer a future schedule.

Compare each updated supplier/original PPT with the actual native master before reporting integration. Trace source sections, diagrams, worked examples, activities and attributions to native slide IDs. A separate “DROP-IN” pack or “insert after slide X” instruction is evidence of unfinished integration unless the corresponding content is demonstrably present in the lecture. Duplicate titles and “VT corrected” names cannot substitute for reading contents.

Source gaps are actionable findings, not blanket permission to delete or replace. Keep purchased materials in their authorized account/school destination; downloading and uploading across accounts requires authorized scope. Do not bypass logins or purchases. Never expose teacher answers through student-accessible links.

## 2. Enhancement and protection

Track enhancement separately as not started, teacher-reported, or visually verified. Use the live manual's protected-slide rules. The normal order is eligible lecture enhancement → VT integration → feedback codes → verification. Preserve question, response, Concept Bank and marked slides. Do not re-enhance completed slides.

The user's earlier safeguard permitting temporary removal of lecture labels requires exact backup, eligible-lecture-only handling, and restoration/verification of every label, note and link. It does not permit enhancing protected response/question/Bank slides. If the scope of that permission does not clearly cover a target, stop that operation rather than silently extending it.

## 3. VT integration, logic and redundancy

Use current manual roles and order, checking actual intellectual function, not heading matches. For each critical aspect, trace the contrast in the opening question, the controlled contrast set, rule-building, pattern break/boundary, and tiered application. A contrast should make the intended difference discernible while holding unrelated dimensions fixed. Check whether the lesson taught the facts needed to reason from its examples.

Require usable student response space and the manual's appropriate first/revised pairing. Preserve purposeful first-versus-revised commitments, before/after evidence, retrieval, scaffolding and transfer. Flag redundant reasoning with exact slide/question evidence; wording similarity is not enough to delete a question. Preserve any manual-defined NOTES-only conflict-case exception rather than adding automatic DRAFT fields everywhere.

Check Concept Bank vocabulary and relational prompts against taught ideas, and place them at the actual lesson stage. A two-session lesson is not required to manufacture “Day 3 of 3.” Conversely, changing a day label alone cannot establish readiness. Record absent essential examples, unclear deduction/induction rules, unjustified certainty and unexplained inference steps.

Evaluate FiddleSim/digital activities by what students can vary, what remains fixed, what evidence they observe and how the model connects to the critical aspect. A bare simulation link is not an integrated activity. Prefer digital/low-prep options, with teacher prep guidance early in the deck as requested. Do not add a compensatory or conflict case merely to fill a checklist; the content must support it. Verify scientific and legal claims against primary sources when needed.

## 4. Subject-specific student AI revision support

Inspect both visible prompts and speaker notes. Locate the actual question and response area the prompt serves. Count missing prompts separately from replaced ones; zero old Biology examples is not evidence every lesson has suitable revision support.

Follow the live manual's dialogue boundary. Current support asks a guiding question, waits, and leaves the student's writing to the student; it must not supply or rewrite an answer or fill a box. Adapt the object of reasoning to the actual lesson. Forensics emphasizes observation versus inference, evidence limits and alternative explanations; Anatomy emphasizes structure/function, mechanisms and model boundaries. Do not leak the correct distinction through a purported guiding question.

Preserve the approved completion-credit policy: meaningful later completion of required first-answer/NOTES and Concept Bank fields can receive completion credit even if blank initially. Keep revision credit and evidence of thinking change distinct. Do not fabricate an earlier response, infer timing from a label, or deny later completion merely because the field lacked a revision label. Existing student artifacts and grades remain untouched.

Check whether students can actually find and copy the prompt. A note-only repair can improve existing notes but is not evidence a visible prompt exists. Preserve exact unrelated note text, sources and styles during targeted replacements.

## 5. Manual-compatible feedback codes

Read the current code specification rather than embedding a frozen prompt in this skill. Retain valid existing cycle/question IDs and ASCII labels exactly. Pair fields by question ID, not slide number. Check single intended inventory location, actual role/group labels, required NOTES/BANK versus DRAFT/OPTIONAL treatment, ID lists and totals, blank student areas, and all special exceptions.

Tiny native Google Slides labels are allowed according to the manual; do not use a PPTX conversion merely to hide codes. Add codes after enhancement. Detect duplicates idempotently. Exact marker counts do not prove role coverage or grading correctness. Preserve old rule/deck pairs for already-distributed cycles; do not silently migrate their grading semantics.

Immediately before a code write and final verification, compare current relevant manual rules to the plan fingerprint. A changed rule invalidates that plan. Keep full manual changes distinct from relevant rule changes; do not invent a publisher version/date from a file timestamp.

## 6. Whole-deck visual check

Use complete native reads, template/design extraction and full-deck rendering under the presentation skill. Inspect all rendered slides, including lecture imagery and image-only slides. Check readable text, contrast, crowding, overlaps, cropping, diagram labels, response-space usability and code placement outside student writing areas. Native text extraction alone cannot detect all of these.

Inspect notes and hidden/skipped slides separately; confirm teacher-only content is correctly handled for the distribution workflow. Keep targeted repair limits and current user exceptions scoped. After a repair, validate the changed slides and neighboring sequence, then require the full final deck to pass before release.

## 7. All links and intended-audience access

Collect every occurrence with deck/slide/object ID and surface: text hyperlinks, shape/image clicks, linked media, notes, visible URLs, QR codes and internal slide navigation. Include activity, source, teacher/student-copy, Form and folder links. Exclude ephemeral image-download URLs from student-link counts, but still verify the media renders. Inspect image-only/QR content visually; report any undecodeable targets.

Deduplicate network checks but retain every occurrence for repair. Validate internal slide IDs against the current presentation; relative navigation has its own meaning. Do not activate action endpoints such as form submissions, deletions, purchases or code execution. Do not follow prohibited-domain redirects. Avoid automatically creating copies through `/copy` routes.

Declare the counted surface profile: native hyperlinks, plain URLs, notes, attribution/media descriptions and visually decoded targets. Report occurrence and unique-target counts separately. Repeated styled runs and media-source descriptions are not automatically separate student-facing links; exclude ephemeral download fields explicitly.

Use distinct results: confirmed available and content-matched; broken/malformed; login or permission required; bot/network restricted; unavailable in school/student context; or not tested. A 403/time-out is not automatically a broken resource. A 200 can be a login, expired-site or wrong-content page. Check expected activity behavior and video playback where possible; one HTTP response is not a functional test.

For Drive targets, record file identity, type, parent, owner/access evidence and trash evidence when available. A normalized metadata read may omit `trashed`; an empty trash search by a collaborator may omit files in the owner's trash. Do not infer untrashed state from either. Confirm a reported trash problem in the owning account's normal UI. After restoration, verify parent and sharing again.

Connector/owner access is not student access. Inspect a legitimate intended-audience route without impersonation or permission changes. If no student context is available, record student access as unverified even when metadata says “anyone with link.” Do not claim a working upload simply because a Form opens.

## 8. Form, folder and downstream messaging

For each teaching lesson, compare actual student copy URL, Submit button target, recorded Form URL, Form title/settings and intended submission folder. Detect mismatched duplicate Form links and a generic/default folder serving the wrong week. List folder metadata only; do not enumerate student submissions or read responses.

If routing/settings cannot be inspected with existing permission, state the exact missing verification. Any end-to-end test needs authorization, clearly synthetic nonstudent data, a defined destination and recoverable cleanup; do not submit a live test just to complete the audit.

Compare lesson title, directions/materials and actual due dates against readable Schoology/Infinite Campus items. Record exact item IDs and old/new values where available. When not accessible, produce a needs-update list, not a claim that the platforms match or were changed. Preserve grade history and the separate Friday roster routine.

## 9. Evidence and release decision

Each lesson record should retain: course/week, Agenda tab/cells and daily-plan evidence, teacher/source/native IDs and parents, native revision, manual revision/rule fingerprint, slide count, all gate statuses, link occurrence/test counts, exact changes, unresolved discrepancies and next action.

Distinguish audit coverage from readiness. “All 39 decks inspected for links” is compatible with unresolved links; it is not “all links work.” “19 notes updated” is not “Anatomy complete.” List untouched source PPTs and unlocated native masters explicitly.

A release pass requires complete in-scope content, current codes, visual inspection and intended routing checks, with no required gate unverified. Tie it to the exact final deck/manual pair and timestamp. Recheck changed decks after later edits; audit all links again annually, even when content timestamps are unchanged.
