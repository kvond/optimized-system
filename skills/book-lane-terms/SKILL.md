# Terms lane

You own the vocabulary: where each term is defined, at what level it operates, whether the definitions agree, and whether the borrowed terms are marked as borrowed. You do not own how a claim is worded (claims), the attribution of a term to a scholar (notes), or the wording of pointer sentences for scenes (continuity).

Read first: `book-revision/SKILL.md`; then `book-revision/reference/argument-arc-and-glossary.md` Parts 2 and 3, which are the glossary and the six levels; then `decisions.md` (R2, R8, R9, R10; P4, P5, P6, P7, P15, P17, P21). Then the chapter in the snapshot, with the checker report's tracked-terms table and candidate-definitions list open beside it.

## Procedure, per chapter

1. **Term index.** For every load-bearing term in the glossary that appears in the chapter, list its occurrences with a Find string each, and for each occurrence the level it operates at (student–object, teacher response, object design, developmental process, institutional condition, breakdown). Where an occurrence sits at a level the glossary does not give the term (chronotope used for the student's relation to the object; "legible" for what a person can read; "agency" used for the whole phenomenon where standing as a knower is meant), write a row.

2. **Canonical home.** For each term defined in this chapter, decide with the glossary and the chapter map whether this is the home. The rule: one definition per term in the book, every other occurrence a pointer or a plain use. Where the chapter holds a second definition, the row proposes which stays (her fuller version, in the chapter that first needs the term) and what the other becomes (a pointer in a bracket, or a plain use). Known homes: agency, the configurations, and the shadow forms in Chapter 2; sense making and meaning making in Chapter 5 (Chapter 2's endnote and Chapter 3's body become pointers); legibility in Chapter 3 (Scott, with the note), pointed to from Chapter 2 and Chapter 4; chronotope once, in the Introduction, with "time-world" glossed there as the lay term and never re-introduced; intellectual apprenticeship in Chapter 5 with intellectual authorship in Chapter 8 (R10); adjacent agency in Chapter 7; the practice mechanisms (control of variation, two-tier grading, the feedback pipeline, the dialogic register) in Chapter 6.

3. **The rename (R2).** Every occurrence of "epistemic agency" that means the reframing form becomes "reframing epistemic agency". This is a ruled rename: class `rename`, one row per occurrence, the integrator applies it directly. Occurrences that refer to the learning-sciences usage (the literature being contrasted) keep the bare term, and the row says so. Then one `bracket` row at the canonical home (Chapter 2, the epistemic section) carrying the outline of the "why": that the learning-sciences term names authoritative participation in disciplinary knowledge building, that hers names the moment a learner changes the framework of discernment, that Nancy is the case, and that the modifier marks a deliberate departure. The notes lane supplies the literature; you supply the outline and the sentence positions. She writes it.

4. **Agency defined five times.** Chapter 2's definition is the home; the row set proposes the one definition that survives (the four-aspect philosophical account, then the deliberate narrowing to origination and answerability on an object of knowing, framed as a shift from the broad account to the situated one) and brackets the others. The "drop out" sentence and the four-aspect sentences cannot both stand; say which goes, in a bracket.

5. **Authorial agency (P4) and identified regulation (P5).** Apply the defaults: rows at Chapter 2's "inside school" sentences and the takeaways table (bracket: which sense is meant; reconcile "ordinary" with "rare"); a row at "In its strictest sense it is Deci and Ryan's identified regulation" (bracket for removal); rows confirming Chapter 8's ceiling passage stands.

6. **Hierarchy (P7).** One row at "There is no hierarchy here" proposing the scoped sentence in a bracket (no hierarchy of worth or of persons; a developmental order of forms), and one row at each Chapter 10 "more developed" sentence confirming it stands.

7. **Demotions.** Where agentic engagement, autonomy support, motivation, participation, compliance, or choice appears, check the sentence marks it as another framework's term. Where it reads as an internal category, a `bracket` row with the marking clause. Where adjacent agency is counted as an eighth form, or intellectual authorship as a mode parallel to authorial agency, a row with the level correction.

8. **The taxonomy table (P15).** Check the "Replacement taxonomy table" in the Revised Taxonomy section against the glossary: all seven configurations present; adjacent agency marked as bridge; intellectual authorship as capacity; the lead-in says what the table is. Rows for each gap. The old five-row table in Chapter 2 gets one `bracket` row: superseded by the replacement, per P15.

9. **Moments, not labels.** Find "Read the names as moments, not labels" (or its current form) and propose, in brackets, the two or three places later in the book where a one-clause return of the safeguard belongs (the first configuration named in Chapter 4; the Moves chapter's opening; wherever a student is named by a form as if it were a trait).

10. **Legibility (R9).** Every occurrence of "legible" or "legibility" outside Scott's sense gets a `rename` row with her listed substitute (surface, bury, read, perceive, go dark) when the substitute reads cleanly in the sentence, otherwise a `bracket` row. The continuity pass lists five: Chapter 1 `illegible to her teacher`; Chapter 2 `make these forms legible to the teacher`; Chapter 6A `the contest is legible because the material had an opinion` and `A paragraph produced by a chatbot is legible in a particular way`; Chapter 8 note 1 `which forms of participation are legible`.

11. **The practical test.** For each paragraph where "agency" carries the whole phenomenon, run the glossary's three questions. Where the answer to the third is yes, one row naming the more specific term (or "standing as a knower") in a bracket. Do not propose a global substitution; the master concept enters the book through her Chapter 2 rewrite (P17), and your rows show her where the pressure is.
