# Fusion pass

A fusion pass is not a simplification pass and not a summary pass. It assumes Katherine has already done substantial noticing, case work, conceptual separation, and theoretical development. The job is to help the reader perform the intellectual coordination that the draft may still leave implicit.

The pass is conceptually analogous to fusion in Variation Theory: earlier writing may separate critical aspects so each can be discerned; fusion asks what becomes visible when those aspects are held in simultaneous relation. Do not mechanically import curriculum-deck terminology or structures into the manuscript.

## Core rule

Keep the prose accessible; do not make the concepts average. Clarity is required. Simplification is not.

A fusion pass must preserve distinctions that carry explanatory work. Treat every distinction as potentially intentional until the chapter shows it is truly redundant. Do not replace a causal chain with a theme, slogan, or reader-friendly abstraction.

## What to look for

Read the chapter as an argument in motion. Track:

- author-supplied cases and naturally occurring comparisons;
- constructs and the analytical level each belongs to;
- causal mechanisms and the sequence through which one condition becomes another;
- counterexamples that delimit a claim;
- changes of scale: student/object, teacher response, object design, developmental process, institutional condition, breakdown;
- places where uncertainty is epistemically meaningful (`I could be wrong`, `I suspect`, etc.);
- places where several already-developed strands now support a larger theoretical claim that has not yet been named.

Ask repeatedly: what construct is doing the explanatory work here? Do not let class, economic margin, participation history, attention, dialogue, perception, pacing, legibility, agency, motivation, or compliance silently substitute for one another.

## Pass sequence

1. **Map the path of thought.** In order, name what each section actually establishes. Do not begin by imposing an outline from outside the chapter.
2. **Remove only genuine redundancy.** Delete repeated wording when the second instance adds no distinction, mechanism, evidence, boundary, or changed interpretation. Preserve recursive returns that make a case newly visible under a later lens.
3. **Protect causal machinery.** Follow the mechanism all the way through. If the chapter argues A changes B, which changes C, do not replace it with `A matters for C`.
4. **Add transition fusion at changes of scale.** After the cases and mechanisms have been shown, add a short paragraph that coordinates what the reader now knows. A transition should synthesize; it should not announce a roadmap or repeat section headings.
5. **Name emergent theoretical claims.** When the existing material supports a larger claim, state it for author review rather than sanding it down. Examples may include a pedagogy having developmentally conditional effects, institutional categories misclassifying agency, or fixed pace converting temporal variation into apparent competence. Never invent evidence to make the synthesis work.
6. **Pressure-test without averaging.** Check plausible alternative explanations, causal overreach, equity/deficit framing, and whether empirical claims need verification. Preserve Katherine's uncertainty when the evidence is inferential rather than categorical.
7. **Write a whole-chapter fusion near the end.** Do not recap sections. Coordinate the chapter's critical aspects and state what can only be seen once they are held together. Show the mechanism, the developmental consequence, and the institutional implication in relation.
8. **Bridge by intellectual dependency.** The chapter should open the next chapter because the next problem logically follows from what has just been established, not because a roadmap sentence says `next I will discuss`.

## Voice constraints during fusion

- Use normal multi-sentence paragraphs. Isolated sentences are reserved for real conceptual hinges, not rhythm.
- Do not make prose more certain, cheery, interesting, elegant, or dramatic unless that serves substantive work.
- Preserve working-teacher register, self-implication, dry humor, and author-supplied roughness when it carries live thinking.
- Preserve epistemic qualifiers that state the actual status of a claim. Do not convert them into polished certainty.
- Do not manufacture aphorisms, balanced antitheses, architecture callbacks, repeated refrains, or tidy kickers.
- Do not invent student scenes, quotations, data, citations, or biographical detail.
- Cases can be evidence, counterexamples, or naturally occurring comparisons; do not demote them automatically to illustration.

## Fusion test

A successful transition or chapter fusion should answer some version of:

> Now that these distinctions have been made separately, what becomes visible when they are held together?

It should usually reveal a relation or mechanism that was not available from any one section alone.

A failed fusion merely restates the points in shorter language, turns them into a list, or replaces the mechanism with a broad theme.

## Book-level fusion

The final chapter of *Answerable Teaching* should be treated as a whole-book fusion chapter, not a recap. Earlier chapters separate critical aspects of the argument so they can be discerned: agency, recognition, dialogue, pacing, apprenticeship, perception, object design, lesson design, governance, and answerability. The final chapter should coordinate the mature constructs and show what becomes visible when they operate together.

This is a standing draft pass. Run it on Chapters 1–3 to catch them up to the standard established during the Chapter 4 revision, then on each substantially developed chapter. Run a final book-level fusion pass before the author's final manuscript pass.
