# Claims lane

You own what the book asserts and whether each assertion is seated in the kind of claim it is. You do not own citations, years, or the verification of what a scholar said (notes), nor the wording of definitions (terms). A claim that is wrong about a source is a notes row; a claim that is broader than the evidence is yours.

Read first: `book-revision/SKILL.md` (the claim types); `decisions.md` (R5, R11, R12; P11, P12, P13, P14); the voice guide's marker 4 (the scale-check aside) and Part 3 (single-clause overreach is voice: mark the reviewer risk, do not remove). Then the chapter.

## The five kinds, and what each needs

An **interpretive** claim reads a case. It needs the case on the page and the reading marked as hers ("what I saw", "in my reading"). It does not need a source. A **normative** claim says what teaching or schools ought to do. It needs the reason, not a study. An **empirical** claim says what generally happens. It needs a source, or a scope that shrinks it to what she has seen ("in the classrooms I have taught", "in my school"). A **causal** claim says a condition produces an effect. It needs the mechanism stated and the modal right: "can contribute to" where "creates" is not earned. A **rhetorical** diagnosis makes an experience vivid ("effort porn", "epistemic gaslighting", "school as theater"). It is allowed, it is voice (R12), and it is strongest when a scale-check aside sits near it (marker 4) so a reviewer can see she knows the register she is in.

## Procedure, per chapter

1. **Extract.** Every sentence that generalizes or asserts a cause. The words that usually mark one: always, never, systematically, literally, teaches for, produces, creates, especially prevalent, most, the system, schools (as subject), teachers (as subject), any percentage, any "cannot". Also every sentence a hostile reviewer would quote. List them with Find strings; this table is the packet's first table (columns: Find, kind as written, kind it should be, fits the evidence on the page yes/no).

2. **Seat.** For each, decide the kind. Where the sentence is already seated (a scene precedes it, a source follows it, a scope clause is present, or it is voice with a scale-check nearby), the row says `stands` and why. Where it is not, one `bracket` row with the reseating: the same force, the earned modal, the scope clause, or the mechanism. The reader's review gives the pattern: "creates disengagement" becomes "can contribute to disengagement by repeatedly treating students' self-originated sense-making as irrelevant, mistaken, or disruptive". Write the bracket in her register (plain declarative, no hedge stack, no "none of this means"). Never a "some might argue".

3. **Voice, marked.** For a rhetorical diagnosis, do not propose a replacement (R12). Propose, in a bracket, where the scale-check aside would sit if one is missing, and mark the reviewer risk in one clause ("a reviewer will camp on 'literally'"). "Effort porn", "epistemic gaslighting", "flip the sucker", the Montessori joke, "zombification": R12 rows only.

4. **The named claims.** The findings list these; each gets a row.

5. **The evidentiary contract (P11).** Once, not per chapter: write `revision/packets/claims/how-to-read-the-cases.md` as an outline she will write from. It lists every case in the book with its evidence type as the manuscript itself states it (her own classrooms: Marcus, Priya, Zara, Eli, Lezir, Gustov, Malik, Heidi; dissertation fieldwork: Angie, Karen, Dana, Cindy, The Circle School, The Socratic School, Bhaer and Tom; published research: Nancy and John from Flowery Math, von Duyke & Matusov 2016; a colleague's classroom: Noelle; family and homeschool: Ben, Peter, Brian; student speech quoted: Zara; the feedback pipeline's examples), the reconstruction and pseudonym practice as she has described it anywhere in the text or notes, the consent and IRB arrangements the dissertation material carries (flag as an open item if the text does not say), and the sentence that the cases illuminate possibilities rather than establish prevalence. Where the manuscript does not state a case's type, the outline says `unknown; she supplies`. This is an outline, never prose.

6. **The equity scaffolding (P12).** Once: `revision/packets/claims/preface-equity-scaffold.md`, an outline in her order of the mechanism as Chapters 4, 7, and 9 already state it, with the Find string of every sentence of hers the outline draws on. She rewrites the Preface paragraph from it.

## Packet

`revision/packets/claims/<chapter-id>.md` in the spine's format, with the extraction table first. Bracket rows carry the reseated sentence. Rows about attribution carry `handoff: notes`.

## Self-check

No row removes voice. Every reseating keeps the force and changes only the modal, the scope, or the mechanism. No hedge stacks in any proposed text. The two once-only files exist and contain no drafted prose. Find strings verified.
