## Reusable operational lessons

- Preserve the learner’s initial and revised answers rather than overwrite the first with the revision. Base feedback and quotations on the actual readable source; do not invent evidence or generic praise.
- Coach one disciplinary question at a time, identify a concrete strength and a gap, and invite the learner to revise in their own words. Keep the learner’s reflective response blank for the learner to complete.
- Separate accessible student feedback, instructor summaries and research coding. Origination and conceptual correctness are distinct judgments; an original but incorrect contribution still warrants attention. Never treat research coding as a grade by default.
- Present one doable next instructional or learning move. Preserve consistency among UI labels, handouts and reports when a contract deliberately changes names. Verify the rendered destination, rather than assuming a specification was deployed.
- When retiring UI functions, preserve verified input feeds, source links, roster access and separate research views until dependencies are intentionally mapped. A lean read/open interface can reduce work without reintroducing retired sending or grading controls.
- Treat authenticity or similarity concerns as review signals, not automatic verdicts or grade penalties. Keep evidence and identifying comparisons private; this summary establishes no detection or messaging authority.
